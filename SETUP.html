⚾ Front Office Baseball — Setup Guide

Put the game on your own free website so friends can join with just a link — no Claude account needed. About 15 minutes, one time.

What you need: a Google account (for Firebase, free) and a free Netlify account (for the website). Keep the unzipped front-office-baseball folder handy — you'll edit one file in it and upload the whole folder.

1Create a free Firebase project

  1. Go to console.firebase.google.com and sign in with Google.
  2. Click Create a project (or Add project). Name it anything, e.g. my-baseball-league.
  3. When it asks about Google Analytics, you can turn it off. Click Create project, then Continue.
You stay on the free “Spark” plan. You never need to add a credit card for this.

2Turn on sign-in

  1. In the left menu choose Build → Authentication, then Get started.
  2. Open the Sign-in method tab. Click Anonymous, switch it on, and Save. (This lets friends join with just a nickname.)
  3. Optional: also enable Google so friends can keep their team when they switch devices. Pick a support email and Save.

3Create the database

  1. In the left menu choose Build → Firestore Database, then Create database.
  2. Pick a location near you (any is fine) and choose Start in production mode. Click Create.
  3. Open the Rules tab. Delete everything there and paste the contents of the file firestore.rules from the folder (open it with Notepad / TextEdit). Click Publish.

4Copy your Firebase settings into the game

  1. Click the ⚙️ gear next to “Project Overview” → Project settings.
  2. Scroll to Your apps and click the </> (Web) icon. Give it a nickname (e.g. baseball). Leave “Firebase Hosting” unchecked. Click Register app.
  3. You'll see code with const firebaseConfig = { ... }. Copy just the lines inside the curly braces.
  4. Open firebase-config.js from the folder in Notepad / TextEdit. Replace the example lines inside { } with yours and save. It should look like:
window.FIREBASE_CONFIG = {
  apiKey: "AIzaSy...your-key...",
  authDomain: "my-baseball-league.firebaseapp.com",
  projectId: "my-baseball-league",
  storageBucket: "my-baseball-league.appspot.com",
  messagingSenderId: "1234567890",
  appId: "1:1234567890:web:abc123"
};
These settings are meant to be public — they identify your project; your database rules (step 3) are what protect it.

5Put the website online (Netlify)

  1. Go to app.netlify.com/drop and sign up / log in (free).
  2. Drag the whole front-office-baseball folder onto the page. Wait a few seconds.
  3. Netlify gives you a web address like https://cheerful-otter-123.netlify.app. Open it — the game should load.
  4. Optional: in Netlify, Site configuration → Change site name to get something nicer like jho-baseball.netlify.app.

6If you enabled Google sign-in: allow your website

  1. Back in Firebase: Authentication → Settings → Authorized domains → Add domain.
  2. Add your Netlify address without https://, e.g. jho-baseball.netlify.app.

7Play!

  1. Open your site, choose a nickname, click + Create Multiplayer League, pick your team (and “Fantasy draft” if you want).
  2. Text your friends the web address. They choose a nickname, open Multiplayer, pick your league and claim a team.
  3. Chat in the Inbox, set who can advance the calendar in Settings, and have fun.

Updating the game later

When you get new game files, drag the updated folder onto your site in Netlify (Deploys tab → drag & drop). Your leagues are safe — they live in Firebase, not in the files. Always keep your own firebase-config.js in the folder.

Troubleshooting

“Couldn't connect to your Firebase project” on the main menu → double-check firebase-config.js (quotes and commas), that Anonymous sign-in is on, and that the Firestore database exists.
Moves don't save / “Missing or insufficient permissions” → re-paste firestore.rules in Firestore → Rules and click Publish.
A friend lost their team after switching phones → nickname-only players are tied to one browser. Enable Google sign-in (step 2 and 6), or the commissioner can free the team in Settings so they can claim it again.
Free limits: Firebase's free plan allows tens of thousands of reads/writes per day — plenty for a group of friends. If you ever hit a limit, the game just pauses saving until the next day.

Single-player franchises work on this site too; they're saved privately per player.
